4. Trivia

September 21 falls in Silver Week (a cluster of Japanese holidays). This typhoon hit during that travel peak: flooding in Kamakura and Chiba, ~200 people queueing for taxis at Shinagawa Station. The trio "heavy rain / violent wind / special warning" returns almost every typhoon season in Kanto.

5. Difficulty notes

  • 接近せっきんする [N3] — to approach (formal news word). Grammar: 〜となっています (result state, N4).
  • 〜にかけて [N3] — toward / through a period.
  • 〜となりそうです [N3] — looks like it will become (conjecture).
  • 最もっとも [N3] — most. 土砂災害特別警報どしゃさいがいとくべつけいほう [N2+ word built from N3 kanji: earth, disaster, special, warning].
  • 夕方現在ゆうがたげんざい [N3+] — news formula "as of this evening". Raw text kept, no simplification.

6. Dialect notes (Standard ⇄ Kansai)

This snippet is fully standard formal news Japanese. In Kansai conversation, 「〜となっています」 becomes 「〜になっとる / 〜なってるで」. A Kansai speaker might say the gist as "It's heavy rain, peak toward the night." Kagawa family speech also uses 「〜しよる」 for ongoing action (e.g. rain falling). For Tokyo daily life, standard + polite form is enough; at home, map standard forms to these Kansai equivalents for listening.
