Translation — open after your read

SHOW ENGLISH TRANSLATION (after your attempt)
  1. One day, a strawberry stood up. "I'm a strawberry!" it said.
  2. The strawberry is standing among white flowers. Around it are lots of green leaves.
  3. Bathing in sunlight, it looked happy. "It's warm here!" said the strawberry.
  4. But the other flowers asked, "Strawberry, why are you standing?" The strawberry thought a little and answered, "I want to become a delicious strawberry! That's why I'll stand!"
  5. Then a white flower laughed and said, "But strawberries are red, aren't they!" At that moment the strawberry was startled.
  6. "Right — wait for me!" it said, and suddenly began turning red. Having turned red, everyone said "Wow, it's a strawberry!"
  7. Happy, the strawberry shouted once more among the flowers, "I'm a strawberry!"

Trivia

Strawberries start as small white flowers and turn red as they ripen — exactly the joke in this story. In Japan, winter-to-spring strawberries are a big seasonal treat, often given as gifts.
TELL ME MORE
White strawberry blossoms become green fruit first, then red when sweet. That is why the flower in the story teases: you cannot be a strawberry while you are still white. Farmers pick them when the color turns fully red.

Difficulty notes

  • [N3] 立(た)つ / 立(た)っています — plain vs te-iru progressive. Standing as an ongoing state.
  • [N3] なりたい (〜たい) — want to become. おいしいイチゴになりたい = I want to become a tasty strawberry.
  • [N3] と言いました / と聞きました / と答えました — quotation with と. Direct speech pattern.
  • [N2+] 浴(あ)びて — sunlight bathing verb; common in weather/nature descriptions.
  • [N3] もう一(いち)度(ど) — once more. Time-frequency expression.

Dialect notes

Standard polite Japanese throughout (〜ました / 〜です). No Kansai forms in this story — compare with the earlier Kansai lesson where sentence-enders and sound words differ. Speech here is soft storybook style with よ / ね / じゃない.
