Trivia

This is a real 2009 Chiebukuro thread about a festival banner in southern Osaka. Someone saw 本間に written huge and bold and started doubting themselves. Phones suggest 本間 because it is a common noun (tatami size / music term), so the typo spreads.
TELL ME MORE
The accepted answer cites the dictionary: ほんま = 本真 (truth), marked as Keihan (Kyoto-Osaka) language and used across western Japan. Related threads debate 本真 vs no-kanji vs 本間, plus そおゆう vs そういう spelling. Great window into how Kansai people actually argue about their own words online.

Difficulty notes

  • ホンマ [N4 listening, N3 reading] — Kansai for 本当. Usually hiragana/katakana, rarely kanji.
  • 本真(ほんま) [N2+ word] — the dictionary kanji (truth). 本間(ほんま) [N3 kanji, wrong word] — tatami-size unit. Classic false friend.
  • 〜して来る人がいます [N4] — people who come doing X (habitual). 〜と思うのですが [N4] — soft question closer.

Dialect notes

Standard says 本当 (hontou); Kansai says ほんま, written in hiragana. Best answer notes Kyoto/Osaka use hiragana by default. Your Kagawa home will say ほんま often, plus 〜しよる for ongoing action and そおゆう-style sound spelling in chats. For Tokyo life, use 本当 / 本当に in writing; recognize ほんま instantly in speech. Never write business mail as 本間に.
